Configure Kueue Resources

With the Kueue operator running, you now create the Kueue resources manually to learn how GPU quota management works. All YAML files are in the kueue-config/ directory and are applied in order.

What You Will Create

You create five sets of resources, each building on the previous one:

  1. Namespaces — Kueue-managed namespaces for training and inference workloads

  2. ResourceFlavors — map Kueue quotas to specific GPU hardware on specific nodes

  3. ClusterQueues — define cluster-wide GPU, CPU, and memory quotas per GPU type

  4. LocalQueues — expose ClusterQueue capacity to specific namespaces

  5. WorkloadPriorityClasses — control queueing order and preemption within Kueue

Architecture Overview

The following diagram shows how the Kueue resources connect to form a complete GPU quota management system. Workloads enter through a LocalQueue, are forwarded to a ClusterQueue for admission and quota checks, and when admitted, the ResourceFlavor injects the correct nodeSelector and tolerations to place pods on the right GPU nodes.

How the Pieces Fit Together

Think of Kueue’s resource hierarchy as a supply chain:

  • ResourceFlavors describe what hardware exists (A100s on worker-1, T4s on worker-2)

  • ClusterQueues set how much of that hardware can be consumed (quotas) and who gets priority (preemption)

  • LocalQueues control who can access the quota (namespace-scoped entry points)

  • WorkloadPriorityClasses determine who goes first when the queue is full

A workload enters through a LocalQueue, is forwarded to the backing ClusterQueue for admission, and if admitted, gets scheduled on nodes matching the ResourceFlavor’s labels.
