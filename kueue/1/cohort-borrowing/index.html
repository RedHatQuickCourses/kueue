Cohort Borrowing and Reclaim

A cohort is a group of ClusterQueues that can share unused quota with each other. When one queue is full, workloads can borrow idle capacity from another queue in the same cohort. This improves GPU utilization — without cohorts, idle GPUs in the training pool sit wasted even when inference is overloaded.

In this chapter, you will:

  1. Configure both ClusterQueues as a cohort

  2. Fill the T4 inference pool and watch a third workload borrow from the A100 training pool

  3. Discover that without reclaimWithinCohort, the lender cannot take its GPU back

  4. Enable reclaim and verify that the lender can evict the borrowed workload

How Cohort Borrowing Works

Key Concepts

  • cohortName — groups ClusterQueues into a shared pool

  • lendingLimit — how many GPUs a queue is willing to lend (protects its own capacity)

  • borrowingLimit — how many GPUs a queue can borrow from the cohort

  • Flavor fungibility — the T4 queue lists a100-gpu as a second flavor; Kueue tries t4-gpu first, then falls back to a100-gpu when T4 is full

What Changes from the Original Configuration

The original 3-cluster-queues.yaml had two isolated ClusterQueues. The cohort-enabled 9-cohort-cluster-queues.yaml adds:

Change a100-cluster-queue t4-cluster-queue

spec.cohortName

gpu-cohort

gpu-cohort

lendingLimit on GPU

1 (lend at most 1 GPU)

Second flavor

Why Not Just Increase the T4 Quota?

You could give the T4 ClusterQueue a quota of 3 GPUs (instead of 2) to handle overflow, but that would permanently reserve an A100 GPU for inference — even when the training pool needs it.

Cohort borrowing is dynamic: the T4 queue only uses A100 capacity when T4 is full and A100 is idle. When the training pool needs its GPUs back, reclaim preemption (covered in Enable Reclaim) returns the borrowed resource automatically. This maximizes utilization without permanently over-provisioning either pool.
