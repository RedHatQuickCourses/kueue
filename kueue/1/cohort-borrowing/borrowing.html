Demonstrate Cohort Borrowing

In this section, you fill the T4 inference pool and submit a third workload that borrows from the A100 training pool. The borrowed pod lands on the A100 node — you can see this in the nvidia-smi output.

References

  • Kueue: Cohort

  • Kueue: Flavors and Resources

  • Red Hat build of Kueue: Using Cohorts

Lab: Trigger and Verify Borrowing

1. Submit inference Deployments to trigger borrowing

  1. Review workloads/deployment-cohort-borrowing.yaml before applying. The file contains three Deployments that all submit to inference-queue. Only the borrowing Deployment is shown here — inference-fill-1 and inference-fill-2 follow the same structure:

    apiVersion: apps/v1
kind: Deployment
metadata:
  name: inference-borrow                               (1)
  namespace: inference-apps
  labels:
    kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name: inference-queue         (2)
spec:
  replicas: 1
  selector:
    matchLabels:
      app: inference-borrow
  template:
    ...
    spec:
      containers:
      - name: inference-workload
        image: registry.access.redhat.com/ubi9/ubi
        ...
        resources:
          requests:
            nvidia.com/gpu: "1"                        (3)
    1 Three Deployments are defined: inference-fill-1, inference-fill-2 (fill the T4 pool), and inference-borrow (triggers borrowing).
    2 All three submit to inference-queue, which routes to t4-cluster-queue. Kueue tries the t4-gpu flavor first.
    3 Each requests 1 GPU. With only 2 T4 GPUs available, the third Deployment exhausts the T4 flavor and Kueue falls back to the a100-gpu flavor — borrowing 1 GPU from the cohort.

  2. Apply the workloads:

    $ oc apply -f workloads/deployment-cohort-borrowing.yaml
deployment.apps/inference-fill-1 created
deployment.apps/inference-fill-2 created
deployment.apps/inference-borrow created

    This creates:

inference-fill-1

1

Fills T4 slot 1 of 2

worker-2 (T4)

inference-fill-2

1

Fills T4 slot 2 of 2

worker-2 (T4)

1

worker-1 (A100)

All three submit to inference-queue → t4-cluster-queue. Kueue admits the first two using the t4-gpu flavor. For the third, the T4 flavor’s quota is exhausted, so Kueue tries the fallback a100-gpu flavor and borrows 1 GPU from the cohort.

    The GPU product name difference is the proof: the borrowed pod sees an A100, not a T4, even though it submitted to the same inference queue as the others. This confirms that flavor fungibility is working — Kueue fell back from t4-gpu to a100-gpu.

  3. Check ClusterQueue borrowing status:

    $ oc describe clusterqueue t4-cluster-queue | grep -A 25 "Flavors Usage" | grep -B 1 -A 1 "nvidia.com/gpu"
      Borrowed:  0
      Name:      nvidia.com/gpu
      Total:     2
--
      Borrowed:         1
      Name:             nvidia.com/gpu
      Total:            1

The first block is the t4-gpu flavor (own quota: 2 GPUs, Borrowed: 0). The second block is the a100-gpu flavor — Borrowed: 1 confirms the third Deployment is using a GPU borrowed from the A100 pool.

Lending is tracked on the borrower’s side, not the lender’s. The t4-cluster-queue shows Borrowed: 1 under its a100-gpu flavor. The a100-cluster-queue does not show a "lent out" counter — Kueue tracks borrowing from the borrower’s perspective. To see that the A100 pool has capacity tied up, check that inference-borrow pod is running on the A100 node (oc get pods -n inference-apps -o wide).
